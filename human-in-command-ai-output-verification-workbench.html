Decision Rights Principle & Specialist Review Flag

AI assistance does not transfer decision authority. The accountable business owner and appropriate human reviewers remain responsible for the decision, documentation, and escalation path.

A Specialist Review Flag is a neutral workflow prompt. It does not identify a legal, technical, safety, privacy, insurance, accounting, or compliance conclusion. It signals that the accountable owner should determine whether an appropriately qualified specialist needs to review before the work continues or is released.

Source Quality Questions

  • Is the source identified?
  • Is the date visible and appropriate?
  • Is the source complete enough for the limited purpose?
  • Is the source authorized for use?
  • Does content require a domain SME review?
  • Does content contain sensitive, personal, health, claim, or financial data? (If yes, abort AI use).
  • Is a specialist review flag needed?
  • Are limitations recorded?

How to Use This Prototype

1. Purpose:

This prototype demonstrates how an organization can organize AI-assisted workflow use while keeping human review, domain expertise, decision rights, evidence, and escalation visible.

2. Demonstration data:

All information is fictional, synthetic, or redacted. Data resets on refresh unless exported.

3. Appropriate use:

Use this prototype to discuss workflow design, management reporting, documentation, training support, responsible automation, reviewer assignment, evidence controls, and follow-up tracking.

4. Not provided:

This prototype does not provide legal advice, legal opinions, employment decisions, payroll calculations, insurance coverage decisions, claim outcomes, compliance certification, safety determinations, technical AI auditing, model validation, bias testing, privacy assessments, cybersecurity assessments, or guarantees.

5. Human review:

AI may assist with limited drafting, organization, summary, comparison, formatting, and stated-rule exception flagging. Accountable human owners and appropriate subject-matter experts review source data, limitations, context, decisions, and external release.

6. Public portfolio data:

Use fictional/synthetic information only in public demonstrations. Do not enter real client, employee, payroll, claim, financial, security, personal, health, surveillance, proprietary, or confidential data.
