AI Hiring Tool Adverse Impact Exposure Calculator

A working four-fifths rule check for AI-assisted hiring, screening, or scoring tools — the same statistical test regulators and courts apply to determine disparate impact.

Why this matters right now: in Mobley v. Workday, Inc. (N.D. Cal.), a federal court allowed disparate impact claims against an AI hiring vendor to proceed — holding that automated screening tools performing traditional hiring functions can trigger the same anti-discrimination exposure as a human recruiter. Disparate impact is unintentional — a policy or tool that is neutral on its face but produces a statistically unequal outcome across protected groups. Disparate treatment is intentional differential treatment. AI screening tools are overwhelmingly an adverse-impact risk, not an intentional-discrimination risk — which is exactly why most employers using them haven't thought to check.

Mobley v. Workday, Inc., Case No. 3:23-cv-00770 (N.D. Cal.) — cited for procedural posture and legal theory only, not as a final determination of liability.
